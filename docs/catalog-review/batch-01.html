Steps

  1. Lie on a flat bench with your eyes roughly under the bar, feet flat on the floor.
  2. Grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder width, unrack it, and lower it under control to your mid-chest while keeping your elbows at about a 45-degree angle to your torso.
  3. Press the bar back up in a slight arc until your arms are fully extended, keeping your shoulder blades pinned together and your glutes on the bench throughout.

Cues

  1. Practise moving the load away from the chest while keeping a stable support position.

Mistakes

  1. Flaring the elbows out to 90 degrees puts unnecessary stress on the shoulder joint and reduces chest involvement.
  2. Bouncing the bar off the chest instead of controlling the descent robs you of tension and increases injury risk.
  3. Letting the hips or feet lift off the bench during the press also reduces stability and power transfer.

Benefits

Practise moving the load away from the chest while keeping a stable support position.

Beginner guidance

Choose a light resistance and a range you can lower without losing your setup.

Steps

  1. Set an adjustable bench to a 30-45 degree incline and sit with a dumbbell in each hand resting on your thighs.
  2. Kick the dumbbells up to shoulder height as you lie back, then press them up and slightly inward until your arms are extended above your upper chest.

  3. Arching the lower back excessively to move more weight is another common compensation to avoid.

Steps

  1. Sit in the machine with your back flat against the pad and grip the handles with a slight bend in your elbows.
  2. Squeeze your chest to bring the handles together in front of you in a wide arcing motion, then slowly return to the stretched starting position without letting the weight stack slam down.

Cues

  1. Trains bringing the upper arms across the torso with limited elbow movement.

Mistakes

  1. Using too much weight often causes people to turn the movement into a press by bending the elbows more and losing the arcing path.
  2. Letting the handles snap back too quickly on the eccentric portion reduces muscle tension and increases strain on the shoulder joint.
  3. Rounding the shoulders forward at the top of the squeeze can also place unwanted stress on the front of the shoulder.

Benefits

Trains bringing the upper arms across the torso with limited elbow movement.

Beginner guidance

Start with a short, comfortable arc and light resistance; a deeper stretch is not a requirement.

Steps

  1. Stand centered between two cable stacks with pulleys set high, grab a handle in each hand, and step forward slightly with a soft bend in your knees and a forward lean at the hips.
  2. Pull the handles down and across your body in an arcing motion until your hands meet or cross in front of your hips, then return under control to the stretched starting position.

Cues

  1. Trains bringing the upper arms across the torso with limited elbow movement.

  3. Setting the pulleys too low (rather than high, for a downward crossing path) changes the muscle emphasis away from the chest.

Steps

  1. Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, grip the barbell just outside shoulder width, and rest it on your front shoulders with elbows slightly in front of the bar.
  2. Brace your core and glutes, then press the bar straight up, moving your head slightly back and then through once the bar clears your face, finishing with the bar directly over your ears and arms fully locked out.
  3. Lower it back to the shoulders under control.

Cues

  1. Builds controlled overhead pressing strength through the shoulders and triceps.

Mistakes

  1. Leaning back excessively to help drive the bar up turns the lift into more of an incline press and stresses the lower back.
  2. Failing to move the head out of the way results in a bar path that drifts forward instead of straight up, reducing efficiency and shoulder safety.
  3. Not fully locking out the elbows or shrugging the shoulders up at the top also limits the strength benefit.

Benefits

Builds controlled overhead pressing strength through the shoulders and triceps.

Beginner guidance

First demonstrate the overhead range without weight; use only a range that stays comfortable.

Steps

  2. Raise both arms out to the sides until they reach roughly shoulder height, leading with your elbows rather than your hands, then lower them back down under control.
  3. Avoid using momentum or swinging the torso to lift the weight.

  1. Trains shoulder strength through an arm-raising motion with a relatively light load.

  3. Locking the elbows completely straight can place unnecessary stress on the elbow joint.

Steps

  1. Stand with feet hip-width apart, the barbell over your mid-foot, and grip it just outside your legs.
  2. Hinge at the hips and bend your knees to grab the bar with a flat back and chest up, then drive through your heels and extend your hips and knees together to stand up straight, keeping the bar close to your body throughout.
  3. Reverse the motion under control to lower the bar back to the floor.

Cues

  1. Trains the posterior chain through a hip-hinge pattern with controlled trunk positioning.

Mistakes

  3. Hyperextending (leaning back excessively) at the top of the lift is another common but unnecessary compensation.

Benefits

Trains the posterior chain through a hip-hinge pattern with controlled trunk positioning.

Beginner guidance

Learn the hip movement with a light load; reduce depth before losing your chosen trunk position.
