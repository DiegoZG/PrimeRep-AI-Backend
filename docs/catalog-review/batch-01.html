Steps

  1. Before loading, have a qualified trainer check the rack, safety supports and spotter arrangement for this bench.
  2. Lie with stable foot and bench contact and take a closed grip on the bar.
  3. Lower the bar in a controlled path, then press it upward without bouncing.
  4. Return the bar to its supports under control; rehearse the setup and return with a trainer before increasing load.

Cues

  1. Keep a closed grip on the bar.
  2. Lower smoothly; do not bounce off the chest.

Mistakes

  1. Flaring the elbows out to 90 degrees puts unnecessary stress on the shoulder joint and reduces chest involvement.
  2. Bouncing the bar off the chest instead of controlling the descent robs you of tension and increases injury risk.
  3. Letting the hips or feet lift off the bench during the press also reduces stability and power transfer.

Benefits

Trains chest and arm strength with a supported barbell press.

Beginner guidance

Have a qualified trainer demonstrate the rack, spotter communication, and safety setup before loading the bar.

Steps

  1. Set an adjustable bench to a 30-45 degree incline and sit with a dumbbell in each hand resting on your thighs.
  2. Kick the dumbbells up to shoulder height as you lie back, then press them up and slightly inward until your arms are extended above your upper chest.
  3. Lower them with control back to just outside shoulder level before pressing again.

Cues

  1. Keep the dumbbells balanced above the forearms.
  2. Maintain contact with the bench as you press.

Mistakes

  1. Setting the incline too steep (over 45-50 degrees) turns the movement into more of a shoulder press and takes emphasis off the chest.
  2. Letting the dumbbells drift too far forward or crash together at the top reduces tension on the target muscles.
  3. Arching the lower back excessively to move more weight is another common compensation to avoid.

Benefits

Trains chest and shoulder pressing strength on an inclined support.

Beginner guidance

Use a manageable pair and get help learning how to enter and leave the bench with the weights.

Steps

  1. Place the hands around shoulder width and support the body on hands and toes with the head aligned with the torso.
  2. Bend the elbows to lower the body as a unit through a controlled range.
  3. Push the floor away to return without letting the hips lag behind.

Cues

  1. Move the torso and hips together.
  2. Keep the head aligned with the body.

Mistakes

  1. Letting the hips sag toward the floor or piking them up removes tension from the target muscles and can strain the lower back.
  2. Flaring the elbows straight out to the sides places excess stress on the shoulders.
  3. Many people also only perform a partial range of motion, stopping well before the chest approaches the floor.

Benefits

Trains upper-body pushing strength while maintaining trunk control.

Beginner guidance

If full push-ups are not controlled, ask a trainer to select a wall, stable elevated-support, or knee-supported variation. Those are different setups, not added weight on this record.

Steps

  1. Have staff adjust the seat, handles or forearm pads for your size according to the machine's instructions.
  2. Take the handles with a comfortable elbow bend and keep that bend consistent as the arms move together.
  3. Return under control through a comfortable range without letting the stack drop.

Cues

  1. Keep the elbow bend consistent through the arc.
  2. Return the handles slowly without letting the stack slam.

Mistakes

  1. Using too much weight often causes people to turn the movement into a press by bending the elbows more and losing the arcing path.
  2. Letting the handles snap back too quickly on the eccentric portion reduces muscle tension and increases strain on the shoulder joint.
  3. Rounding the shoulders forward at the top of the squeeze can also place unwanted stress on the front of the shoulder.

Benefits

Trains bringing the upper arms together against machine resistance.

Beginner guidance

Ask staff to fit the seat and starting position to the specific machine before adding resistance.

Steps

  3. Stand and return the bar to the rack using the practiced sequence.

Cues

  1. Keep the bar supported on the front of the shoulders.
  2. Maintain the rack position while standing up.

Mistakes

  1. Letting the elbows drop during the squat causes the torso to lean forward and the bar to roll off the shoulders, which is the single most common technique breakdown.
  2. Insufficient wrist or shoulder mobility often leads people to grip the bar in a way that strains the wrists rather than truly resting it on the shoulders.
  3. Rushing depth without adequate ankle and hip mobility can also cause the heels to lift or the back to round.

Benefits

Trains leg strength while supporting a barbell in a front-rack position.

Beginner guidance

Have a trainer teach a comfortable front-rack setup and rack return before loading.
