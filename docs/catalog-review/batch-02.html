Steps

  1. Sit in the leg extension machine with your back against the pad and the ankle pad resting just above your feet, knees bent at 90 degrees.
  2. Extend your legs by straightening your knees until they're fully extended, squeezing your quads at the top, then lower back down under control to the starting position.

Cues

  1. Trains knee-extension strength with the thigh supported.

Mistakes

  1. Using too much weight often leads to using momentum — swinging the legs up quickly and letting them drop rather than controlling the movement.
  2. Lifting the hips off the seat to help extend the legs reduces the isolation benefit of the exercise.
  3. Locking the knees out forcefully at the top under heavy load can also place unnecessary strain on the knee joint.

Benefits

Trains knee-extension strength with the thigh supported.

Beginner guidance

Adjust the machine to your leg length and choose a comfortable range at light resistance.

Steps

  1. Lie face down on the leg curl machine (or sit, depending on machine type) with the ankle pad positioned just above your heels.
  2. Curl your legs by bending your knees to bring your heels toward your glutes, squeezing your hamstrings at the top, then lower back down under control to full leg extension.

Cues

  1. Trains the hamstrings by bending the knee against resistance.

Mistakes

  1. Lifting the hips off the pad to help curl the weight up is a very common compensation once the weight gets heavy, and it significantly reduces hamstring isolation.
  2. Using a fast, jerky tempo rather than controlling both the lifting and lowering phases reduces the training effect.
  3. Not achieving a full stretch at the bottom of each rep also limits range of motion benefits.

Benefits

Trains the hamstrings by bending the knee against resistance.

Beginner guidance

Use a short controlled range initially and increase it only while the pelvis remains steady.

Steps

  1. Stand holding a dumbbell in each hand at your sides (or bodyweight only).
  2. Step forward with one leg and lower your body until both knees are bent at roughly 90 degrees, with your back knee hovering just above the floor.
  3. Push through your front heel to stand up and step forward into the next lunge with the opposite leg, continuing to alternate as you walk forward.

Cues

  1. Builds leg strength while controlling one side of the body at a time.

Mistakes

  1. Letting the front knee travel excessively far forward past the toes, or letting it cave inward, are both common technique breakdowns, especially as fatigue accumulates late in a set.
  2. Taking steps that are too short results in a narrow, unstable lunge with excessive forward knee travel.
  3. Leaning the torso too far forward also shifts stress away from the glutes and onto the lower back.

Benefits

Builds leg strength while controlling one side of the body at a time.

Beginner guidance

Practise a shallow bodyweight version and use a stable support if balance limits control.

Steps

  1. Sit on the ground with your upper back resting against a bench and a barbell positioned across your hips (padded for comfort), knees bent and feet flat on the floor about shoulder-width apart.
  2. Drive through your heels to raise your hips up until your torso is roughly parallel to the floor and your body forms a straight line from shoulders to knees, squeezing your glutes hard at the top, then lower back down under control.

Cues

  1. Builds hip-extension strength with the upper body supported.

Mistakes

  1. Hyperextending the lower back at the top of the movement rather than stopping at a straight torso-to-knee line is a very common error that shifts stress away from the glutes and onto the spine.
  2. Not driving the hips high enough (partial range of motion) reduces the training stimulus.
  3. Letting the knees cave inward during the drive up also reduces glute activation and stability.

Benefits

Builds hip-extension strength with the upper body supported.

Beginner guidance

Learn to finish with the hips extended without trying to lift the ribs higher.

Steps

  1. Stand on the calf raise machine with the balls of your feet on the platform and your shoulders under the pads, heels hanging off the edge.
  2. Lower your heels down as far as comfortable to feel a stretch in your calves, then rise up onto your toes as high as possible, squeezing at the top, before lowering back down under control.

Cues

  1. Builds calf strength through controlled heel raising and lowering.

Mistakes

  1. Using a very short range of motion — small, bouncy pulses rather than a full stretch-to-contraction range — significantly reduces the training stimulus.
  2. Bending the knees during the movement shifts emphasis away from the gastrocnemius.
  3. Rushing through reps without pausing at the top or bottom also reduces time under tension for this notoriously stubborn muscle group.

Benefits

Builds calf strength through controlled heel raising and lowering.

Beginner guidance

Begin on a flat surface and use support before adding load or extra depth.

Steps

  1. Get into a forearm plank position with elbows directly under your shoulders and your body forming a straight line from head to heels.
  2. Engage your core, squeeze your glutes, and hold the position, breathing steadily, without letting your hips sag or pike upward.
  3. Hold for the prescribed time.

Cues

  1. Trains deliberate trunk control while the limbs or external resistance challenge position.

Mistakes

  1. Letting the hips sag toward the floor is the most common error and places excess strain on the lower back rather than the core.
  2. Piking the hips up too high reduces the effectiveness of the exercise by taking tension off the abs.
  3. Holding your breath instead of breathing steadily throughout the hold is another common mistake that limits how long you can maintain good form.

Benefits

Trains deliberate trunk control while the limbs or external resistance challenge position.

Steps

  1. Sit on the rowing machine with feet strapped in and grip the handle with both hands.
  2. Drive through your legs first, then lean back slightly and pull the handle to your lower ribs, finishing with your arms bent and elbows tucked.
  3. Reverse the sequence — extend your arms, lean forward, then bend your knees — to return to the starting position and repeat rhythmically.

Cues

  1. Supports rhythmic whole-body activity at an individually chosen effort.

Mistakes

  1. Pulling primarily with the arms instead of driving power from the legs first is one of the most common and inefficient rowing errors.
  2. Rounding the lower back, especially as fatigue sets in during longer intervals, can create unnecessary spinal stress.
  3. Rushing the stroke rate without focusing on power per stroke also reduces overall efficiency and can lead to premature fatigue.

Benefits

Supports rhythmic whole-body activity at an individually chosen effort.

Beginner guidance

Start slowly and learn the equipment controls before increasing speed or resistance.
